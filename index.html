Welcome to KnitTogether'27
The 2nd European CL Research Summit for Junior Researchers

What is KnitTogether?

KnitTogether is a junior researcher retreat where we want to spend a week together with young researchers from the field of NLP and, with fun and creativity, work on a specific topic. LLMs are having an ever-growing influence on society. We therefore think it is important to critically reflect on them and on our own research, and to consider whether and how we can do research in the interest of society.

The Idea of KnitTogether

We want to get to know each other in a non-competitive environment. This is not about “proving” that you are a good scientist. Rather, we want to think together about the developments of LLMs, collect new ideas for research projects, and meet people with whom you can connect beyond the meeting and perhaps meet again at conferences or work together in the future.

Appreciative communication and an appreciative atmosphere are especially important to us. It is not about “performing”, criticizing each other, or comparing ourselves with one another. We want to learn from and with each other, share experiences, and above all have fun. We want to spend time together – discussing and developing ideas, but just as much eating, playing games, making music, and everything else that goes with it.

You are in the right place if ...

  • you would like to meet people from your research area and exchange ideas with them.
  • you value a respectful, appreciative atmosphere and science without pressure to perform.
  • you would like to share experiences from your PhD and talk with others about challenges (e.g. difficulties with supervision, lack of feedback, time management, or other challenges of everyday academic life).
  • you are currently doing your PhD or are possibly in an early postdoc phase.

Content

We want to examine the societal, cultural, and political implications of LLMs and, in doing so, also critically question our own role as researchers: What responsibility do we bear in researching, developing, and using LLMs?

The thematic focus of this edition will be “Interpretability for Social Good”. How can we use interpretability to make societal assumptions, discriminatory patterns, representation gaps, and systemic inequalities in LLMs visible? What possibilities do different methodological approaches offer for identifying and changing problematic structures? We are interested both in methods for analyzing and explaining LLMs and in approaches that make it possible to specifically examine and change internal representations and model behavior.

We would like to consider as broad a range of interpretability approaches as possible (e.g. feature attribution, probing, mechanistic interpretability, causal interventions, model editing, or steering). We are open – so if you feel addressed or are planning to move in this direction with your research, you are welcome.

What Happens at KnitTogether?

This is only the second edition; you can find the website of the first edition here: KnitTogether'25. We develop the program partly based on the input we receive from the participants. KnitTogether is not a summer school; this means there may be a few lecture-style formats such as 1–2 keynotes, but most of the program is interactive. Nobody is forced to do anything – anyone who needs a break or does not feel comfortable can withdraw at any time.

The “peer-to-peer” format was especially well received in the first edition. Participants prepared short inputs (e.g. presenting a method, a tool, or a dataset, or giving a theoretical input). In smaller groups, you could then get different inputs depending on your interests. Also popular was the conference game: in small teams, you worked out a small research project together and wrote an abstract. Just for fun, experiment results were simulated with dice, and you could also collect a few hidden “grants”.

In general, though, there will be a bit of everything: 1–2 keynotes by renowned researchers on the topic of interpretability for social good, mini-tutorials, discussion rounds on content-related and meta topics, mentoring sessions for advice, idea rounds and pitches, and of course plenty of room for games and fun.
